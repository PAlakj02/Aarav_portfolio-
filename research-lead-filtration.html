Comparative Evaluation of Lead Removal from Contaminated Water by Chemical Precipitation Using Different Reagents under Varying pH and Dosage Conditions

This study was designed to investigate the removal of Pb by chemical precipitation at different pH and precipitant dosage using sodium carbonate (Na2CO3), sodium sulphate (Na2SO4) and sodium hydroxide (NaOH). Experiments were conducted at pH 5, 7, and 9, with dosages of 0.10, 0.25, and 0.50 g/50 mL, using an initial Pb concentration of 53.58 mg L−1.

The removal efficiency was found to be quite different for various precipitants and operating conditions. The overall performance of Na2CO3 was the best, with a mean of 92.77% removal of lead. NaOH showed comparatively lower removal under the tested conditions.

Chemical precipitation was found to be effective in removing lead from contaminated water, and Na2CO3 was found to be most effective under the conditions studied.
