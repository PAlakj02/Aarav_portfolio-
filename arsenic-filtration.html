Low Cost Portable Device to filter Arsenic and other heavy metals.

Design Patent application no-202531130896.

The Problem

My grandparent’s hometown – Assam is known as the cancer capital of India. In the villages I came across people with skin lesions, patchy skin, lung diseases and some even had gangrene. On enquiring further, I found out that the ground water has beyond permissible levels of arsenic and other heavy metals like iron and lead.

During my internship with Prof Robin Dutta (Tezpur University, Assam) I understood the feasibility of arsenic filtration and its on-ground challenges — the existing methods were expensive, built for community systems or were difficult to implement.

The Device

The handheld portable and manually operated water filtration device is designed to remove arsenic, heavy metals and turbidity from contaminated groundwater or surface water.

It integrates the principles of the Arsiron Nilogon method with a novel piston-driven filtration mechanism contained within a single, compact bottle.

To be used by students, travellers, defence personnel operating in arsenic or heavy metal-affected remote areas.

How It Works

The Arsiron Nilogon method uses oxidation-coagulation-adsorption (OCOP) process. The system comprises a bottle-like cylindrical plastic container that holds ½ litre to 1 litre of the contaminated water. Treatment begins with the addition of sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) to adjust the pH, potassium permanganate (KMnO4) to oxidize arsenite {As(III)} to arsenate {As(V)}, and ferrous iron to insoluble ferric oxides. The added ferric chloride (FeCl3) to form ferric oxide-hydroxide flocs. These flocs adsorb and co-precipitate arsenic, co-precipitate other heavy metals and removes turbidity through coagulation-sedimentation. The resulting sludge settles at the bottom of the container.

Once sedimentation is complete, a piston assembly is manually pushed into the container. The piston is fitted with a vertical sand–gravel filter pipe, an inlet pipe connected to the top of the filter pipe, an outlet pipe connected to the bottom of the filter pipe. These three components — the filter pipe, inlet pipe, and outlet pipe — are fixed to the piston and move vertically as a single unit.

Results

The bottles were tested with water from 20 different tubewells. All were well below WHO drinking water guidelines.

97.8%Arsenic reduced
94.4%Iron reduced
96.8%Turbidity reduced
